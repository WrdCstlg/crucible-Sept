### Ruthless Synthesis: Why `branch_1` Survives and `branch_2` Fails

#### 1. Algorithmic Efficiency & Space Complexity
* **`branch_1` (Closed-form State Machine):** Resolves business minutes algebraically. By calculating the overlap of business hours mathematically in closed form ($O(1)$ per state transition), it eliminates intermediate collections entirely. State advancement operates strictly in-place against ticket state, preserving register locality and zeroing heap allocations.
* **`branch_2` (Event Sweep & Interval Reconstruction):** Degenerates into a two-phase materialization pipeline. First, it sweeps events to construct dynamic interval objects in memory; second, it iterates over those intervals to count minutes. This incurs an $O(N)$ allocation penalty per ticket where $N$ is the number of state changes, introducing unnecessary garbage collection pressure and cache misses.

#### 2. Scaling Profile under Production Load
While micro-benchmarks show identical zero-second runtimes (`Duration: 0.0s`) on trivial test payloads, `branch_2` introduces an unmitigated scaling hazard:
* Large ticket histories with frequent pauses/resumptions force `branch_2` to construct and traverse long interval chains, turning an analytical time-delta operation into an unbounded iterative sweep.
* `branch_1` advances ticket state deterministically. Regardless of the span between status transitions, the computation cost remains constant.
