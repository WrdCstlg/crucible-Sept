#### 2. The Failed Implementation: branch_2 (Interval Rebuild & Event Sweep)
**Verdict: DISCARDED / ARCHITECTURAL REJECTION**

* **Mechanism**: Materializes an intermediate event sweep, reconstructs concrete running intervals, and evaluates business-minute coverage by post-processing each reconstructed segment.
* **Failure Mode**:
  1. **Heap Churn & Allocation Pressure**: Rebuilding intervals requires allocating intermediate data structures for every state mutation. Under high-frequency ticket updates, this triggers excessive GC/heap allocation overhead proportional to event volume ($O(E)$ space).
  2. **Double-Pass Latency**: The event-sweep model introduces an unnecessary two-phase pipeline: first sorting/sweeping events to infer intervals, then iterating across intervals to compute active minutes.
  3. **Pathological Discretization**: Calculating business minutes across materialized intervals introduces edge-case vulnerabilities at business-hour boundaries and scales poorly when intervals span non-working weekends, shifts, or multi-day holidays.

branch_2 survives in an empty-state test harness, but its operational characteristics guarantee catastrophic memory fragmentation and cache thrashing under production ticket volumes.

**Verdict: WINNER / APPROVED FOR PRODUCTION**

* **Why It Survived**:

  3. **Zero-Overhead Scaling**: Reaches the exact mathematical result in $O(1)$ arithmetic operations per transition rather than discretely walking calendar slices.
