## Ruthless Synthesis

### Why `branch_1` Survived
`branch_1` models the problem correctly as a localized state machine backed by **closed-form business-minute arithmetic**:

1. **Deterministic Asymptotic Complexity:** By calculating business time analytically between transition points (using modular day-arithmetic and pre-computed schedule offsets), `branch_1` resolves elapsed business minutes in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time per state transition and $\mathcal{O}(1)$ working memory.
2. **Zero Allocation Pressure:** It eliminates intermediate timeline representations. Timestamps are consumed inline, mutating the accumulator directly on the ticket record.
3. **Cache Line Locality:** Tickets and their operational state remain compact and contiguous. No auxiliary heap structures are instantiated during evaluation.

### Why `branch_2` Failed Architectural Standards
Despite returning `Exit Code: 0`, `branch_2` is an anti-pattern destined to collapse under high-volume streaming data:

1. **Two-Pass Allocation Overhead:** Rebuilding explicit "running intervals" requires materializing timestamp tuples onto the heap for every state toggle. At scale (millions of ticket updates across distributed shifts), this introduces massive allocation churn and GC pressure.
2. **Double-Handling Compute Cost:** First it sweeps and re-sorts/reconstructs intervals ($\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$ or dynamic slice appending), then iterates through each interval to compute business minutes. This multi-pass design introduces memory fragmentation, poor cache utilization, and branch mispredictions.
3. **Failure to Exploit Invariants:** Business calendars and operating hours are fixed, cyclic invariants. Materializing intervals and traversing them discretely ignores closed-form calendar arithmetic, turning a constant-time math problem into an unbounded collections-processing bottleneck.

## Architecture Directive
Commit **`branch_1`** to trunk. **`branch_2`** is permanently shelved. No implementation that materializes discrete intervals for static-schedule SLA calculations will pass architectural review.
